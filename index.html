How it works

Daily habits — tap the big circle on a card to check the habit off for today; tap again to undo. The flame shows your current streak: consecutive scheduled days completed. Today doesn't break the streak until it's over.

Weekly-target habits — for things like working out, where it's the count that matters, not the day: set a target such as 3× per week and check days off whenever they happen. The card's ratio shows this week (e.g. 2/3) and the streak counts weeks in which you hit the target; the running week never breaks the streak until it's over.

Start/stop habits (intermittent fasting) — the same button does both: tap in the evening to start the fast, and tap when you eat again. While fasting, the card shows the live elapsed time; after stopping, it counts the eating window and shows the clock time your next fast is due to keep the rhythm (24h minus your goal), falling back to the last fast's length once a day has passed. A fast running past 24 hours turns amber and asks “still fasting?” — tap the timer to fix a forgotten stop. A fast counts for the day it ends on. Set a fasting goal when editing the habit: a ring fills around the button as the fast progresses like a traffic light — red for the first third of the goal, yellow for the middle third, green for the home stretch — while the card counts down how long is left and shows the clock time (24-hour) at which you'll hit the goal (e.g. “2:30 left, goal 11:30”). Everything turns green when you reach it.

Editing a fast — forgot to press start or stop on time? While fasting, simply tap the running timer number on the Today card to correct the start time. All fasts can also be edited from the detail view's list (including the one in progress) — start, end, or delete.

The fasting card's tap areas — faint dashed lines on the fasting card mark its separate touch zones: left of the first line is the start/stop button, the middle opens the habit's details, and — while a fast runs — right of the second line the timer number fixes the start time. The lines are only a hint; the zones themselves are unchanged.

Week dots — the M–S dots show this week (Monday first); filled dots are completed days, dimmed dots are days the habit isn't scheduled, and the ratio (e.g. 5/7) is completed / scheduled this week.

Fixing the past — in a habit's detail view, the month calendar is editable. For daily and weekly habits, tapping a day cycles it: done → skip day → empty. A skip day (shown hollow with a dashed outline) is excused — it neither breaks nor extends a streak, and it leaves the completion count — perfect for holidays or sick days. For fasting, tapping an empty day asks how long the fast was (entering 0 marks a skip day); tapping a day with a recorded fast opens that fast in the editor, where you can adjust its start and stop, delete it, or cancel — nothing changes until you save. Use the arrows to move through months — up to a year ahead, so a planned vacation is visible; future days are locked except vacation-marked skip days, which a tap un-marks. Streaks and stats update immediately.

Vacations & sick weeks — under the month calendar, “Skip several days” marks a whole date range as skip days at once: pick the first and last day (future dates included, up to 92 days), and tick “Apply to all habits” when the whole life pauses, not just one habit. Days already done stay done, and the Undo bar takes back exactly the days the range added. A planned range shows up on the calendar right away and can be checked in the months ahead.

Forgot yesterday? — press-and-hold the check circle of a daily or weekly habit to mark yesterday done, without opening the calendar. A short Undo bar confirms it (or tells you yesterday was already done). A normal tap still checks off today. Backfilled days never trigger milestone celebrations.

Week in review — each Monday, a “Last week” card appears under the habit list on the Today screen: per habit, how many scheduled days (or weekly-target check-offs) you managed, how your streak changed over the week (green +, red −), and for fasting the number of fasts with the average length and how it compares to the week before. Tap a row to jump to that habit; the dismisses the card until the next week starts, and Settings → “Show week in review” brings it back any time. It's pure summary — nothing about your data changes.

Fasting stages — the fasting detail view shows the commonly-cited phases of a fast on a colored track: Digestion (0–4h), Settling in (4–12h), Fat burning (12–16h), and Deep fast (16h+). While a fast is running, a marker sits at your elapsed time, the current stage is highlighted, and a line names it (“Now: Fat burning”); it moves along live. This is a popular approximation from fasting literature, not medical advice — every body is different.

The fasting calendar — for a fasting habit, each calendar day shows the date with the fasted hours in a circle beneath: green when that day's goal was met, gray when the fast fell short of it, red with a live count for today's fast in progress, and a dashed circle with a dash (–) for skip days. The Fasts card counts how many days met the goal.

Day notes — tap the pencil in the calendar header to enter note mode, then tap any day to write a short note (“skipped, travel day”, “hard fast”). Days with notes get a mark, and the month's notes are listed under the calendar; tap one to edit it.

Tonight's goal — press-and-hold the fasting habit's start button to pick a goal for just this fast (13/15/16/18h or the habit's default) before it starts. You can also change the running fast's goal by tapping it in the detail list. The ring, countdown, and goal time follow the per-fast goal.

Details — tap anywhere else on a card for the year-by-year pixel grid, current and longest streak, and completion %. Small letters mark where each month begins, and tapping any pixel shows that day's date, status, and note. Timer habits also list recent fasts with average and longest duration.

Year poster — the share button next to a year heading in the detail view turns that year into a poster image: the habit's icon and name, all 365 days as a month-by-month pixel grid in the habit's color (skip days dashed), and the year's headline stats underneath. On iPhone it opens the share sheet, so the picture can go straight to Messages, Photos, or Instagram; elsewhere it downloads as a PNG. Past years get their own poster with days-done totals.

Undo — starting or ending a fast, and removing entries, can be taken back for a few seconds via the Undo bar at the bottom of the screen, so a stray tap never costs you anything.

Gestures — swipe the month calendar left or right to change months, and drag any bottom sheet downward to close it. The Today header shows a progress bar of how many scheduled habits are done today, and the settings, stats, and add buttons float at the bottom of the screen, within thumb reach.

Reorder, layout & theme — Settings → Reorder habits moves habits up and down the Today list; Settings → Layout switches between the list view and a two-column grid; Settings → Theme picks auto (follow the system), always light, or always dark.

Icon badge — on an installed app, the home-screen icon shows how many scheduled habits are still open today. It updates whenever the app is opened or used (a web app can't change it in the background).

Archiving — done with a habit but want to keep its history? Archive it from its detail view: it disappears from Today but keeps everything, and can be restored any time from Settings → Archived habits. Deleting, by contrast, is forever.

Adding & editing — ＋ creates a habit with a name, icon, color, and type; the icon picker offers 40 hand-drawn icons and a search field that understands English and German words (try “kaffee” or “sleep”). Daily habits can be limited to certain weekdays. The pencil in the detail view edits it (the type is fixed once created).

Stats — the chart button on the Today screen opens an overview of all habits: current and best streak, this month vs last month, and the last 30 days as a pixel strip per habit.

Milestones — hitting a round streak (7, 14, 30, 50, 100… days, or 4, 10, 26, 52 weeks) or setting a personal-best fast earns a short celebration. Each milestone fires once.

Fast chart — the fasting detail view charts your recent fasts as bars against the dashed goal line, so the trend is visible at a glance; tap a bar to edit that fast.

Voice commands (Siri) — as of iOS 2026 releases, Shortcuts cannot open an installed web app: the “Open App” action doesn't list it, and “Open URLs” lands in Safari, which keeps a completely separate copy of your data — so a voice or Lock Screen launcher isn't possible yet. The practical setup is the app icon in the Dock; the icon badge shows the day's status before you even tap. Worth re-trying after each iOS update: if “Tracking” ever appears in the Shortcuts “Open App” picker, a “Hey Siri, Fasten” shortcut and a Lock Screen button will work immediately. (The ?fast=start/stop/toggle web commands exist for platforms where links open in the installed app.)

Updates — the app checks for a new version when opened and shows an “Update” button at the bottom when one is live; one tap loads it, no force-quitting needed. Settings → Check for app update asks immediately.

Your data — everything is stored only on this device. Export opens the share sheet on iPhone, so the backup can go straight to iCloud Drive (elsewhere it downloads a file); Import restores it. Settings shows how long ago the last backup was — since the phone holds the only copy, an occasional backup is worth it — and the app asks the system to protect its storage from cleanup (the status is shown below). “Export CSV for Excel” shares two spreadsheet-ready files (semicolon-separated, decimal commas): one row per logged day with status and note, and one row per fast with start, end, and duration.
